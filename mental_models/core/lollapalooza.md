# Lollapalooza

> *"Three or four forces all coming together produce nonlinear effects."* — Munger

## Summary

Multiple independent forces aligning produce results greater than the sum of parts. Great business + great CEO + fair price + tailwind is the *combination*, not any single factor. The lollapalooza is what makes a multibagger; missing one ingredient is what makes a "good but not great" outcome.

## When to apply

- High-conviction labelling — require alignment across ≥3 independent dimensions
- Cross-framework analysis — F1 (quality) + F3 (innovation) + F6 (platform) aligning is a different signal than any in isolation
- Sizing decisions — concentrated positions justified only when multiple forces align, not when one is exceptional
- Risk assessment — *negative* lollapalooza is also real (multiple structural problems compounding)

## Inversion form

Single-factor blindness: anchoring on one strong signal and ignoring the others. F7 strong_fit (cheap) by itself is not lollapalooza — it's one factor. AMBA dive: F7=7 strong_fit but F4=3 fail and F5 ROIC negative — single-factor cheap, no lollapalooza, value trap.

## Antipatterns

- "Great CEO, that's all I need" / "great moat, that's all I need" — single-factor monomania
- Combining 3 closely correlated signals and calling it 3-factor agreement (e.g. P/E low + P/B low + EV/EBITDA low — all valuation, one factor)
- Ignoring negative lollapalooza when multiple frameworks fail simultaneously
- Treating overlay bonuses as additively unbounded (would let single overlay capture cap)

## Examples in this codebase

- INTA dive: F3=8 + Rule of 40 = 41 + ICA-5 upward + GP/A 0.42 — multiple independent forces aligning constructively
- HIMS dive: F2 fail + F4 fail + asset growth +205% (CGS disqualifier) + ICA-5 downward — negative lollapalooza
- Composite scoring renormalises across feasible frameworks — agreement among three feasible frameworks beats agreement among two
- ADR-023 §1.3 Block 2 mandates the narrative LLM identify lollapalooza alignment explicitly

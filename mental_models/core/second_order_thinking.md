# Second-Order Thinking

> First-order: revenue grew 30%. Second-order: at what unit-economic cost, what cohort behaviour is hidden in the headline, what does churn say about year three?

## Summary

Past the first observable layer, every metric has implications that determine whether the first-order read is durable. Revenue growth is fine; revenue growth funded by accelerating customer-acquisition cost at flat retention is bad. The headline lies, the second order tells.

## When to apply

- Every growth metric — what's the unit economic delta behind it?
- Every quality metric — is the trajectory improving or deteriorating?
- Every "great year" — was it sustainable or pulled forward?
- Every "bad year" — was it temporary or structural?

## Inversion form

First-order-only thinking: surfacing a headline metric without its second-order implication. Sell-side prose lives here ("revenue beat by 5%"). Defence: every framework signal pairs a level with a derivative or a context ratio.

## Antipatterns

- Revenue growth without unit-economic context
- ROIC level without trajectory
- "Beat earnings" without checking whether SBC was excluded
- Free cash flow without checking working-capital gymnastics

## Examples in this codebase

- F1 P4 (accruals quality) — OCF > NI is the second-order check on first-order earnings
- F5 ROIC + R&D intensity + GP/A — multi-dimensional capital efficiency, not single metric
- HIMS dive: revenue +59% YoY (first-order good) BUT asset growth +205% (second-order = CGS disqualifier)
- ICA-5 trigger fires on the *gap* between GAAP and non-GAAP ROIC, not on either level — the divergence IS the second-order signal

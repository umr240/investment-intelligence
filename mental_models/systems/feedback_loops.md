# Feedback Loops (Reinforcing vs Balancing)

> Reinforcing loops compound. Balancing loops stabilise. Most "great businesses" are reinforcing loops; most "stable businesses" are balancing.

## Summary

Systems-thinking foundation. A reinforcing loop amplifies its own output (network effects, brand investment, scale economies). A balancing loop dampens its output toward equilibrium (competition, regression to mean, regulatory pushback). Most multibaggers ride a reinforcing loop; most failures involve a reinforcing loop in reverse (vicious cycle).

## When to apply

- Every moat narrative — what feedback loop reinforces this moat? What balancing loop limits it?
- Every "compounding" thesis — identify the specific reinforcing mechanism, not just the historical compounding rate
- Every recession analysis — which balancing loops engage? Do reinforcing loops survive the cycle?
- Every "vicious cycle" risk — multiple negative feedbacks compounding into terminal decline

## Inversion form

What does a *reinforcing loop in reverse* look like? Brand decay → fewer customers → less marketing budget → less brand → fewer customers (Sears, JCPenney). Identifying the inversion requires mapping the loop in both directions.

## Antipatterns

- Citing "compounding" without identifying the reinforcing mechanism
- Treating balancing loops as failures rather than as natural stabilisation (some companies are stable, not declining)
- Ignoring the feedback structure when one strong year happens — was it a reinforcing-loop kick-in or a one-time shock?
- Assuming reinforcing loops continue indefinitely (every loop has a balancing counterpart eventually)

## Examples in this codebase

- F6 Helmer Network Economies — explicit reinforcing-loop moat detection
- DCF declining-variable-cost trend — reinforcing economies of scale
- INTA dive read: vertical SaaS switching costs are a reinforcing loop (more years of accumulated configuration → more switching costs → longer customer life)
- HIMS dive: marketing-led growth at 39% intensity — the loop is fragile because customer-acquisition cost rises with scale (balancing loop dominating)

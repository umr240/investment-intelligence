# Entropy (Moats Decay)

> The default state of every moat is decay. Maintaining position requires sustained energy input. The exceptions are the businesses worth owning.

## Summary

Thermodynamics' second law translated to business: in any open system without active maintenance, order decays toward disorder. Moats erode under competitive pressure, brands drift toward irrelevance, organisational capital diffuses to competitors via employee mobility. The companies that compound are those whose moat-maintaining mechanisms exceed the entropy rate.

## When to apply

- Every moat narrative — what's actively counteracting entropy here, and is that counter-mechanism strengthening or weakening?
- Long-tenured-business analysis — has the moat been maintained or has it slowly eroded?
- "Brand strength" analysis — brand is a high-entropy asset; what marketing investment is preserving it?
- Patent / regulatory moats — finite-duration; the entropy is structural

## Inversion form

The over-corrective stance: assuming all moats decay at the same rate. Some moats are genuinely durable for decades (regulated franchises, network effects with high multi-homing cost). The discipline is calibrating the decay rate to the moat type, not assuming uniform fast decay.

## Antipatterns

- Treating a "great moat" as if it requires no maintenance investment
- Citing 30 years of strong returns without checking whether the moat-maintenance investment ratio has crept upward (entropy taking hold)
- Patent moats treated as permanent (they expire by design)
- Ignoring management-quality decay — even strong cultures drift across CEO transitions

## Examples in this codebase

- DCF terminal-value perpetuity-growth assumption is bounded because entropy precludes infinite-horizon stability
- Moat direction analysis defaults to "narrowing" if no widening evidence is present — entropy bias built into the lens
- Tier 1 ROIC volatility filter detects entropy creeping into capital efficiency
- HIMS dive: marketing intensity of 39% is the anti-entropy expense for the brand; if this rises further while revenue growth slows, entropy is winning

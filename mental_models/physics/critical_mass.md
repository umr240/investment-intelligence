# Critical Mass

> Below the threshold, the system isn't self-sustaining and dies under perturbation. Above the threshold, the system becomes self-reinforcing.

## Summary

Physics concept (nuclear fission, nucleation, percolation theory) applied to network-driven businesses: below a critical scale the network can't sustain itself and decays toward zero; above critical mass the network becomes self-reinforcing and grows under its own dynamics. The key analytical challenge is identifying which side of the threshold a business is on.

## When to apply

- Network-effects business analysis — has this business achieved critical mass in its network?
- Marketplace analysis — both sides of the marketplace need critical mass independently
- Platform-defensibility analysis — Helmer Network Economies Power requires post-critical-mass status
- Pre-IPO / early-growth analysis — has the business crossed critical mass, or is it still subsidising both sides?

## Inversion form

The "almost there" trap: a business near but below critical mass can look identical to one above it on a cross-sectional snapshot. The difference is dynamic: the below-threshold business decays without subsidy; the above-threshold business grows without it. Defence: trace customer-acquisition cost trajectory and unit-economic trends through the threshold.

## Antipatterns

- Network-effects claimed without checking critical-mass status
- Treating pre-critical-mass growth as evidence the business will compound (it won't unless the threshold is crossed)
- Ignoring multi-homing — multi-homing changes where the critical-mass threshold is, often elevating it dramatically
- Confusing scale (general size) with critical mass (specific dynamic threshold)

## Examples in this codebase

- F6 customer concentration analysis (Patatoukas) is one signal of network density / critical-mass status
- F6 founder-CEO ownership signal correlates with stage — pre-critical-mass companies often still have heavy founder ownership
- AMBA dive: specialty silicon doesn't have a network-effects component to apply critical-mass to — different moat type
- HIMS dive: telehealth-DTC has no network effect requiring critical mass — the customer base is independent (each customer doesn't benefit from others)

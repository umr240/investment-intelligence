# TAM Ceiling

> A perfect moat inside a $500M TAM produces a niche business, not a multibagger. Always ask what the company looks like at 30% market share.

## Summary

Moat without runway is a niche business. The relevant compounding question requires both: durable competitive position AND a ceiling high enough to compound into. A 50%-share company in a $500M TAM has nowhere to go even if its moat is perfect.

## When to apply

- Every multibagger thesis — what's the TAM, and what does this company look like at 30% share?
- Niche-business identification — strong moat + small TAM = good single-decade compounder, not 10x-er
- Cross-checking growth narratives — "huge TAM" claims in the deck are usually overstated; scrutinise definitions
- Sector context — software TAMs grow faster than industrial TAMs; treat them differently in compounding projections

## Inversion form

Two failures: **TAM-claim inflation** (CEO claims $X trillion TAM that's a creative aggregation of unrelated markets) and **TAM-collapse blindness** (TAM shrinks underneath the company — taxi medallions, photographic film, in-person retail). Inversion test: if the company is at 30% share five years out, what's the outcome? If trivial, the TAM is too small or the moat doesn't matter.

## Antipatterns

- "Massive TAM" claimed without segmentation analysis
- Conflating addressable with serviceable (SAM ≠ TAM)
- Treating a high-quality niche business as if it had compound runway
- Ignoring TAM collapse — newspaper companies pre-Craigslist still looked like moat businesses by historical metrics

## Examples in this codebase

- F6 Rule of 40 — combines growth (a TAM-driven signal) with profitability (a moat signal) — neither alone is sufficient
- INTA dive read — vertical SaaS for legal/PE has narrower TAM than horizontal SaaS, but density of switching costs makes compounding possible within that TAM
- AMBA dive read — specialty silicon TAM is real but Mag 7 dominance compresses serviceable share toward niche
- HIMS dive read — DTC telehealth TAM is large but unprotected (incumbents enter cheaply), so TAM ≠ runway in the absence of moat

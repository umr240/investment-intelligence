# Red Queen Effect

> *"It takes all the running you can do, to keep in the same place."* — Lewis Carroll, via Van Valen.

## Summary

Evolutionary biology: in a coevolutionary system, every adaptation by one species is met by counter-adaptations from competitors. Standing still in absolute terms requires constant investment. Translated to business: companies in highly competitive sectors must continually reinvest just to maintain market position; failing to run is failing to stay.

## When to apply

- High-R&D / high-marketing companies — is the spending producing position GAIN, or just maintaining?
- Tech sector analysis — Red Queen dominates; semiconductor incumbents must invest at every node
- Consumer brand analysis — Red Queen via marketing intensity (HIMS at 39% marketing intensity)
- Pharma R&D — must continuously refresh pipeline as patents expire

## Inversion form

The Red-Queen-misread failure: treating maintenance investment as growth investment. A company spending heavily on R&D to maintain ROIC at 15% looks identical to one spending heavily to compound ROIC from 15% → 25% — until the cost-structure derivative is examined. Defence: track ROIC trajectory + variable-cost trend, not just R&D dollars.

## Antipatterns

- Celebrating R&D growth as innovation when it's just Red Queen running
- Treating high gross margin as moat evidence in sectors where Red Queen requires high gross margin just to fund the run
- Ignoring competitive R&D race dynamics — your company's R&D growth means little if competitors' is faster
- Confusing maintenance capex with growth capex

## Examples in this codebase

- F3 innovation framework: R&D intensity + GP/A together — Red Queen alone is high R&D; widening moat is high R&D + GP/A expansion
- DCF cost-structure regression with declining variable cost trend — *winning* the Red Queen race
- AMBA dive: high R&D intensity (Red Queen running) but ROIC negative both GAAP and adjusted = running and losing ground
- INTA dive: R&D heavy AND ROIC trajectory positive = running and gaining ground (winning the Red Queen race)

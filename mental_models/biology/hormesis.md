# Hormesis (Stressors Strengthen)

> Sub-lethal stress strengthens. Selye's biology, Taleb's economics. The discipline is exposing systems to enough stress to strengthen, not enough to break.

## Summary

Biological principle: organisms exposed to controlled stressors develop adaptive responses that leave them stronger than unstressed counterparts. Translated: businesses that have been tested by recessions, competitive entries, technology shifts often emerge with stronger balance sheets, leaner cost structures, and more loyal customer bases than untested peers.

## When to apply

- Recession-stress analysis — has this business been *tested* through a downturn, and how did it emerge?
- CEO evaluation — has this leader managed through a crisis? How?
- Recent-IPO context — these companies haven't been hormetically stressed yet; selection pressure unobserved
- Multi-cycle moat durability — the moat that survived 2008 and 2020 is different from the moat that's only existed in expansion

## Inversion form

The over-stress failure: stressors beyond capacity break the system rather than strengthening it. Hormesis has a dose-response curve; too much is fatal. Defence: the stressor must be sub-lethal *and survived*. Companies that went bankrupt in 2008 weren't hormetically strengthened — they're the dead sample.

## Antipatterns

- Treating "weathered the storm" claims at face value without verifying what specifically was weathered
- Confusing "didn't go bankrupt" with "emerged stronger" — surviving isn't the same as strengthening
- Ignoring recent-IPO companies' lack of hormetic exposure when scoring durability
- "Battle-tested" rhetoric without specific cycles named

# Liking / Loving Tendency

> Building a research thesis creates emotional investment. The screener exists partly to short-circuit this.

## Summary

Munger's category for the disproportionate cognitive favour given to people, products, companies we like. In research, the act of thesis-building itself creates liking — the more time spent, the more attached. The bias is structural, not character-flaw, and requires structural defences.

## When to apply

- Every dive — am I evaluating the business or defending my growing investment in the thesis?
- Every "great CEO" read — would I evaluate this neutrally if I'd never read the founder's interview?
- Every "great product" reaction — does this matter to the unit economics, or am I just a customer?
- Every counter-evidence — am I weighting it appropriately, or am I rationalising past it?

## Inversion form

The cynical over-correction: refusing to like anything to "stay objective." Genuine enthusiasm about quality is appropriate; the discipline is having symmetric weighting between liking-induced positive evidence and inversion-induced negative evidence.

## Antipatterns

- "I love this product" as part of an investment thesis without unit-economic support
- CEO charisma weighting heavier than capital allocation track record
- Defending a name after new contradictory data because of accumulated affection
- Excluding companies whose CEOs come across as unsympathetic in interviews despite strong Outsiders trait scores

## Examples in this codebase

- Composite scoring is universe-comparable — forces every name to compete against alternatives, not stand alone in the warmth of the analyst's attention
- Verdict vocabulary forbids emotional language (ADR-023 §1.5)
- F4 stealth framework rewards LOW analyst coverage — structural defence against the consensus-popularity trap
- ADR-023 §1.3 Block 4 mandates plain direct voice; bans "compelling", "attractive", etc.

# Kondratieff Long Waves

> Schumpeterian innovation S-curves at the multi-decade scale — railways, electricity, automobiles, semiconductors, AI. Each wave creates and destroys industries.

## Summary

Nikolai Kondratieff and Joseph Schumpeter's framework: innovation produces multi-decade waves of economic transformation. Each wave has a leading technology, a build-out phase, a maturation phase, and a stagnation phase before the next wave begins. Investing inside the leading-technology wave produces different base rates than investing in mature waves.

## When to apply

- Sector-classification — which long-wave is this sector in? Build-out, mature, or post-peak?
- Long-horizon thesis durability — Kondratieff context informs whether the multi-decade compounding window is open or closing
- Innovation-thesis evaluation — F3 frameworks should weight differently in early-wave vs late-wave sectors
- M&A consolidation patterns — late-wave sectors often see industry consolidation (textile rollups in 1900s, oil consolidation in 1990s)

## Inversion form

The "this wave is different" trap: assuming the current technology wave (AI, biotech, etc.) won't follow the historical pattern of build-out → maturation → stagnation. Some waves do compound longer than others, but the *pattern* tends to recur. Defence: anchor wave-relevant assumptions against historical wave durations and trajectories.

## Antipatterns

- DCF perpetual-growth at early-wave growth rates (treats wave continuation as guaranteed)
- "AI is different" without explicit reasoning about why this wave's curve differs from prior tech-wave curves
- Pattern-matching to railways → 1990s tech without checking which similarities are structural vs cosmetic
- Ignoring that late-wave incumbents often look strong right before being displaced by next-wave technology

## Examples in this codebase

- AMBA dive: specialty silicon in mid-late-wave semiconductor industry → unfavourable competitive structure
- INTA dive: vertical SaaS in early-mid-wave software industry → favourable reinvestment runway
- HIMS dive: DTC consumer health in mid-wave consumer-internet industry → competitive entry from incumbents intensifies as wave matures
